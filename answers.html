Answers Atlanta owners ask

Is a filter wash the same as a hood cleaning?

No. Filters can be soaked by the kitchen. The job fire marshals and insurers usually ask about is the hood, plenum, accessible grease duct, and exhaust fan cleaned down to bare metal.

How often should we book it?

NFPA 96 ties the inspection to cooking load, not a flat Atlanta calendar. Solid-fuel cooking is monthly. High-volume, 24-hour, charbroiler, or wok cooking is quarterly. Moderate volume is semiannual. Churches, seasonal kitchens, and day camps are often annual. An insurance policy can be tighter. Confirm with the fire marshal who inspects your address.

Why is one quote much higher?

Restaurant owners comparing quotes have described a higher price that includes documentation and a lower price that does not mention it. Hood length, horizontal duct above a ceiling, fan count, roof access, and months since the last clean also change the hours. Get the scope in writing before you pick the smaller number.

What should be on the sticker?

Date, company name, and the areas cleaned. Ask for a separate note of anything they could not reach — missing access panels and a fan that would not hinge are the usual gaps.

Is the suppression tech the same visit?

Often no. Fusible links and nozzle service follow a semiannual suppression schedule. A clean hood in January does not mean the nozzles were checked in June. Ask which company owns which date.
